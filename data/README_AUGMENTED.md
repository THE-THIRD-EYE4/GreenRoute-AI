## Why this augmentation exists

The shipped SU-02 dataset had five structural defects that made the stated
problem unsolvable as given. Each is repaired here.

| ID | Defect in the original | Repair |
|----|------------------------|--------|
| D1 | `Cost_per_km_per_ton` = 0.075 and `CO2_per_ton_km` = 0.105 on **all 45 routes**. Cost and carbon were perfectly collinear, so "balance cost against carbon" had a single answer. | Four transport modes with conflicting economics, plus a per-lane fleet tier. Ground-mode cost/CO₂ correlation drops from 1.00 to **+0.21**, and **88% of lanes now carry ≥2 non-dominated options**. |
| D2 | `Transit_Time_days` = 1 on every route, including 350 km ones. | Recomputed from distance, mode speed, duty-cycle hours and terminal dwell. Now spans 1–6 days. |
| D3 | 7 suppliers had no route at all; 13 of 24 customers were unreachable; **54% of daily demand had no feasible path**. | Full connectivity generated. **15/15 suppliers, 24/24 customers, 100% of demand reachable.** |
| D4 | `Inventory_Risk` = LOW on all 4,050 rows. Shipment `Status` = DELIVERED on all 600. `Route_Status` = AVAILABLE on all 45. Nothing to classify, nothing to detect. | Risk derived from days-of-cover and safety-stock ratio after a seeded demand shock. Shipment and route statuses given realistic class distributions. |
| D5 | `digital_twin_state` warehouse rows had **no `Product_ID`** (75 rows = 5 × 15, order-implied only), and the snapshot was timestamped 2026-09-18 while the data ended 2026-08-29. | Twin rebuilt from the repaired ledger with an explicit `Product_ID`, six node types, and the timestamp reconciled to 2026-08-30. |

## The decoupling, explained

This is the part to put on a slide, because it is the heart of the project.

Freight carbon and freight cost are not naturally in conflict — a slower, fuller
truck is usually both cheaper and cleaner. The conflict appears along **two**
specific levers, and both are modelled here:

**Lever 1 — mode choice.** Rail is cheap and clean but slow. Air is fast but both
costly and dirty. Electric road transport costs *more* per ton-km than diesel
while emitting roughly a third as much. That last pair is the genuine
cost-versus-carbon trade-off.

| Mode | Cost coeff | kg CO₂/ton-km | Speed | Duty hrs/day | Terminal dwell | Lane eligibility |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Truck_Diesel | 0.0750 | 0.1050 | 55 km/h | 8 | 0 d | any |
| Truck_Electric | 0.0985 | 0.0335 | 48 km/h | 7 | 0 d | ≤ 420 km (range) |
| Rail | 0.0355 | 0.0280 | 32 km/h | 20 | 2 d | ≥ 280 km, both ends rail hubs |
| Air | 0.8900 | 0.6020 | 620 km/h | 24 | 1 d | ≥ 650 km, both ends have airports |

CO₂ factors are order-of-magnitude consistent with GLEC Framework v3 and DEFRA
2023 freight factors. Cost coefficients are kept on the original 0.075 scale so
no downstream rescaling is needed.

**Lever 2 — fleet tier.** Each lane is assigned Legacy / Standard / Modern.
A Legacy fleet is cheaper to hire (×0.86) and burns more (×1.26); a Modern
Euro-VI fleet costs more (×1.19) and emits less (×0.79). This produces a
cost-versus-carbon conflict *within* a single mode, which is what keeps the
Pareto front wide even on short lanes where only trucks are eligible.

Because air is genuinely both expensive and dirty, the all-mode correlation stays
high (+0.90) and that is correct, not a bug. The headline figure is the
**ground-mode correlation of +0.21**.

## Chargeable weight

`parcels.csv` precomputes this because it is standard freight practice and gets
mispriced constantly:

```
chargeable_weight = max(gross_kg, volume_cm3 / divisor)
    air divisor  6000
    road divisor 3000
```

A light, bulky pallet is billed on volume, not mass. Your quote engine must use
chargeable weight for pricing and gross weight for vehicle payload limits. They
are different numbers and confusing them is the commonest costing error in
logistics software.

## Load-dependent emissions

Do not use a flat per-ton-km factor for road legs once you are routing actual
vehicles. Use:

```
CO2_kg = distance_km × (EF_Empty + (EF_Full − EF_Empty) × load_factor)
```

An empty rigid 12t truck still emits 0.62 kg CO₂/km against 0.95 kg fully loaded.
That is why **consolidation and load factor are the biggest carbon levers in road
freight**, and why your VRP should optimise utilisation, not just distance.
Almost no hackathon project models this. It is your differentiator.

## Limitations to state upfront

1. Supplier reliability, disruption probability and all delay behaviour are
   **simulated**. Real supplier performance data is not publicly available.
2. Road distances are haversine × 1.29, a standard Indian detour factor, not
   routed road geometry. Swap in OSRM or OpenRouteService for real distances.
3. Air schedules, rail frequencies and the vehicle fleet are representative,
   not actual published timetables or a real carrier's fleet.
4. Emission factors are class averages. Real factors vary with gradient,
   payload distribution, driver behaviour and ambient temperature.
5. The digital twin is simulated from this dataset. A production twin needs live
   ERP, telematics and carrier EDI feeds.
6. Scope is capped deliberately (15 suppliers, 4 modes, 168 lanes, 24 customers)
   to stay tractable, as the brief advises.
